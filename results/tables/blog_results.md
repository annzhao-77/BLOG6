The common sample contains **143 father-child pairs and 123 distinct fathers**. The selected income-year age is **36** for both generations. It maximizes the number of pairs eligible for both models among ages 30–45, before any regressions are estimated. Age is approximated by survey age minus one.

| Measure for both generations | Estimated IGE |
|---|---:|
| Single year at age 36 | 0.220 |
| Average of available incomes at ages 29–43 | 0.297 |

The average uses a mean of 6.4 observed income years for fathers and 5.1 for children. Fathers contribute 3–9 observations and children contribute 3–8. The mean elapsed span from first to last observation is 8.1 years for fathers and 8.3 for children. Observation counts and elapsed spans are different: missing years are not filled in.

The multi-year point estimate is higher than the single-year estimate in this sample. A 1% difference in fathers' average income is associated with approximately a 0.297% difference in children's average income. This is a descriptive association, not a causal effect.
